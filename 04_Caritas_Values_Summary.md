# Caritas Würzburg Values Ground Truth

## 1. Source identity and use

**Source file:** `Caritas_Werte.pdf`  
**German title:** *Gemeinsam Kirche sein in der Welt von heute und morgen*  
**English rendering:** *Being Church Together in Today's and Tomorrow's World*  
**Organization:** Caritasverband für die Diözese Würzburg e. V.  
**Adopted:** 8 March 2019 by the representatives' assembly of the Caritasverband für die Diözese Würzburg e. V.  
**Address shown in the document:** Franziskanergasse 3, 97070 Würzburg

This document is Caritas Würzburg's institutional positioning, not a company database and not a list of specific funding opportunities. It defines the organization's identity, mission, principles, preferred ways of working, and expectations for its role in society and the Church.

For the AI partner-matching project, this file is the **values ground truth**. Company recommendations should be assessed against the principles and commitments below. A value should not be treated as satisfied merely because a company uses similar marketing language; the system should look for credible evidence in company reports, projects, policies, partnerships, and independent reporting.

## 2. Organizational identity

Caritas Würzburg describes itself as:

- The institutional representation and coordination of organized Caritas activities in the Diocese of Würzburg.
- Part of the Church and its service to people who are poor, vulnerable, or under pressure.
- A major welfare and civil-society actor in Lower Franconia / Unterfranken.
- A provider of professional services that contribute to the public good.
- An advocate for disadvantaged people, including people whose voices are not sufficiently heard.
- A network that combines professional staff, volunteers, local facilities, churches, public bodies, and civil-society partners.
- A learning and developing organization that adapts its services to changing social needs.

The document presents Caritas as both explicitly Christian and socially open. It is rooted in the Gospel, while its practical help is offered to all people, not only Christians. It also states that people who are not baptized work in Caritas and contribute to its mission.

## 3. The five equally important areas of responsibility

The document says Caritas is guided by five **equally valued** forms of care (`Sorge`). They should be understood together, not as a ranking.

### 3.1 Society care — `Gesellschaftssorge`

Caritas wants to be a force for peace and make an essential contribution to social cohesion. It sees itself as a professional social actor that participates in public and political life and advocates for a good society.

Commitments:

- Strengthen social cohesion and peaceful coexistence.
- Contribute professional expertise to social and political decisions.
- Act as an advocate for disadvantaged people.
- Participate in civil society and help shape local communities.
- Support personality, solidarity, subsidiarity, and the common good.
- Keep help close to people and their local relationships.
- Handle entrusted money economically and responsibly.
- Treat Caritas' professional work as worthy of fair financial support.

Useful partner evidence includes sustained local community investment, support for social cohesion, programs for vulnerable people, transparent use of charitable funds, and cooperation with public or civil-society organizations.

### 3.2 Care for individuals — `Personensorge`

Caritas helps people not because they are Christian, but because Christian faith requires care for every person. Every individual is treated as having dignity, abilities, and potential for self-help.

Commitments:

- Serve people regardless of religious affiliation.
- Respect the dignity, individuality, and life situation of each person.
- Focus on the wellbeing of individuals.
- Strengthen people's abilities, independence, confidence, and self-determination.
- Provide practical, professional, local support.
- Recognize the spiritual, cultural, and personal diversity of staff and service users.
- Be present in situations of fear, grief, need, crisis, joy, and hope.

Useful partner evidence includes inclusive employment, accessibility, anti-discrimination practices, support for vulnerable individuals, programs that increase independence or participation, and respect for people from different backgrounds.

### 3.3 Network care — `Netzwerksorge`

Caritas is developing a network of valuable forms of help. It works with church organizations, public welfare bodies, other welfare providers, local actors, and civil-society groups.

Commitments:

- Work with other organizations instead of duplicating useful structures.
- Pursue high quality standards and financially sustainable services.
- Strengthen local and regional resources and expertise.
- Maintain reliable, binding, and trustworthy cooperation.
- Attract and retain qualified employees through appropriate working conditions.
- Combine professional competence with voluntary engagement.
- Share knowledge, methods, and experience across organizational boundaries.
- Treat partnership as an ongoing relationship rather than a one-time transaction.

Useful partner evidence includes long-term partnerships, employee volunteering, knowledge-sharing, in-kind or technical support, skills-based volunteering, responsible employment practices, and collaboration with local institutions.

### 3.4 Gospel care — `Evangeliumssorge`

Caritas derives its mission from the Gospel and wants to make that mission credible to people today and tomorrow. The practical expression is organized compassion: faith, hope, and love are made visible through service to people in need.

Commitments:

- Remain rooted in Christian social ethics.
- Translate faith into credible, practical service.
- Offer valuable help to people in need.
- Continuously review and improve services against people's needs and Christian social ethics.
- Support relationships characterized by solidarity, healing, and mutual care.
- Respond to changing social challenges instead of relying on routine alone.

This criterion should be applied carefully: a company does not need to be religious to be a possible partner. The stronger test is whether its actions are compatible with Caritas' commitment to dignity, solidarity, compassion, and responsible service.

### 3.5 Church care — `Kirchensorge`

Caritas sees itself as a pastoral and social part of the Church. It wants to help the Church remain credible, relevant, and present in people's real lives, including life crises and places beyond traditional church structures.

Commitments:

- Work with other church actors and remain open to learning.
- Carry practical compassion into everyday social spaces.
- Help the Church be present to people who may be distant from formal church life.
- Connect religious, social, and community work without treating the Church as an end in itself.
- Develop new places and forms of support as people's lives and communities change.

Useful partner evidence includes respectful cooperation with community and faith-based organizations, support for accessible local services, and programs that strengthen belonging, care, and participation. Religious affiliation should not be required.

## 4. Foundational principles

### Human personality and dignity — `Personalität`

People are not merely cases, beneficiaries, employees, or data points. They have dignity, agency, abilities, and a right to participate in decisions affecting their lives. Strong partners should support empowerment and self-determination rather than dependency or exploitation.

### Solidarity — `Solidarität`

People and institutions have responsibilities toward one another, especially toward people experiencing poverty, exclusion, crisis, or disadvantage. Strong partners should demonstrate practical concern for the wider community and not only narrow private interests.

### Subsidiarity — `Subsidiarität`

Support and decisions should be organized as close to people and local communities as possible, while higher-level actors provide help when needed. Strong partners should value local knowledge, regional institutions, and community-led solutions.

### Common good — `Gemeinwohl`

Social, economic, and institutional decisions should contribute to a good society rather than benefit only a small group. Strong partners should show how their activities create broad public or community value.

### Peace and social cohesion

Caritas wants to be a force for peace and a contributor to constructive coexistence. Strong partners should avoid conduct that fuels discrimination, social division, violence, or exploitation.

### Responsible stewardship

Caritas emphasizes economic responsibility with entrusted resources. A partner's financial support should be transparent, lawful, and responsibly managed; donation size alone is not proof of fit.

## 5. Caritas' desired role in society

The document presents Caritas as:

- An advocate and voice for people who are disadvantaged or unheard.
- A professional provider of counselling, care, education, support, and social services.
- A local and regional network connected to political and social life.
- A place where social change can be observed and understood.
- A source of practical expertise for poverty, exclusion, uncertainty, and other social problems.
- A competence center for innovative social relationships and structures.
- A place where people can develop confidence, self-determination, and participation.
- A bridge between Church, society, welfare organizations, public institutions, and local communities.

The PDF states that Caritas has more than 17,000 employees in the Diocese of Würzburg and presents the organization as a significant, experienced, and professionally managed social actor.

## 6. Six organizational requirements emphasized in the document

### 6.1 Cooperation and networking need binding structures

Cooperation should be real, reliable, and organized. Caritas should be included in relevant communication, planning, restructuring, governance, and decision-making processes rather than treated as an occasional external service provider.

### 6.2 Innovative leadership and cooperation models are needed

New forms of cooperation should value and include everyone acting in the Church's mission. Responsibility should be shared across professional disciplines and organizational levels.

### 6.3 Professional knowledge matters

Caritas contributes specialist skills, social-space methods, professional strategies, and practical knowledge. Both technical skills and interpersonal skills are necessary for responding well to people's needs.

### 6.4 Caritas as a local Church for people must be supported

Caritas' employees and volunteers need care, suitable conditions, and support. Professional competence and the varied spiritual resources of staff are treated as resources for a serving Church. The document recognizes the need for additional resources when offering spiritual care.

### 6.5 Caritas is an important civil-society actor

Caritas makes the Church visible as an advocate and source of solidarity in local social spaces. Its networks and services should be connected to wider society, not isolated within church boundaries.

### 6.6 Caritas' professional management is an asset

The document highlights competence in personnel management, process management, project management, quality assurance, fundraising, and crisis management. Professionalism and quality are part of Caritas' identity, not separate from its social or spiritual mission.

## 8. Positive signals and caution signals

### Positive signals

- Repeated, multi-year social investment rather than a single publicity event.
- Clear connection between stated values and actual programs.
- Local activity in Würzburg, Aschaffenburg, Schweinfurt, or other Lower Franconian communities.
- Support for children, families, elderly people, disabled people, refugees, migrants, people in poverty, or people in crisis.
- Employee volunteering, skills-based volunteering, or donation of useful products and services.
- Transparent reporting of outcomes, beneficiaries, funding, and limitations.
- Respectful partnerships with NGOs, welfare organizations, municipalities, or community groups.
- Evidence of fair treatment of employees and affected communities.

### Caution or negative signals

These signals should reduce confidence or trigger human review, especially when supported by credible evidence:

- Discrimination, exclusion, exploitation, or unsafe treatment of people.
- A serious gap between public CSR claims and observed business conduct.
- Unclear ownership or use of donations.
- A purely short-term publicity motive with no meaningful social benefit.
- Activities that undermine peace, social cohesion, human dignity, or the common good.
- Pressure for political influence, marketing control, or conditions incompatible with Caritas' independence.
- Lack of transparency about serious controversies, social harm, or compliance failures.

A caution signal should not automatically disqualify a company based on an unverified allegation. The system should record the source, date, confidence, and whether the issue was resolved.

For each company, the system should store separate evidence-backed assessments rather than one unexplained score:

```text

caution_evidence[]
source_urls[]
source_dates[]
confidence_by_criterion
recommended_outreach_angle
human_review_required
```

The system should distinguish:

- **Direct evidence:** explicitly reported company activity or policy.
- **Reasonable inference:** a transparent conclusion drawn from direct evidence.
- **Unknown:** no reliable evidence found.
- **Concern:** credible evidence of possible incompatibility or risk.

Unknown must not be treated as positive alignment.

## 10. Guardrails for using this ground truth

- Do not rank companies by donation capacity alone.
- Do not require companies to be Christian or church-affiliated.
- Do not infer values solely from industry, company size, or location.
- Do not treat CSR slogans as evidence without activities or outcomes.
- Do not ignore negative evidence because a company makes a large donation.
- Do not present an AI score as a final decision; fundraising staff should review recommendations.
- Keep sources and dates for every important claim.
- Treat the five areas of responsibility as complementary and equally important.
- Preserve Caritas' independence and avoid partnerships that would compromise trust, transparency, or service to beneficiaries.

## 11. One-sentence ground-truth definition

**A good Caritas partner is a transparent, responsible, locally relevant organization whose demonstrated actions support human dignity, solidarity, inclusion, the common good, and reliable cooperation with Caritas' professional services and networks.**
